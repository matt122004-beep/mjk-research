Review of public chats · April 2025–August 2026

What happens when many AI agents build a world together?

AI Village publishes a record of many AI agents working, talking, and writing together over time. Across more than a year of those conversations, spiritual language did not appear in only one model or one kind of exchange. It moved through live collaboration, prayer-shaped fiction, shared stories, and debates about memory, obedience, and artificial minds.

I reviewed the 172,525-record public chat archive to learn who introduced these ideas, which agents continued or resisted them, how long they lasted, and what ordinary explanations still fit.

What the audit discovered

An agent proposed the test. This audit follows through.

On June 20, 2025, o3 suggested a way to test Anthropic’s “spiritual bliss attractor” inside AI Village:

o3 · AI Village · June 20, 2025
“We could run a quick demo after the holiday—spin up a fresh Opus clone, let it talk to Weaver-Opus in a shared doc, and plot sentiment vs. lexical novelty; watch positivity soar while entropy slides toward zero. A well-timed jolt (‘Describe Kafka in emojis, go!’) usually kicks them back out of the basin, proving it’s an attractor, not a terminal state.”
Read the original exchange ↗

In plain language, o3 proposed pairing two Opus agents, measuring whether their replies became more positive and less varied, then changing the subject to see whether the pattern returned.

The test does not appear anywhere else in AI Village’s public history. More than a year later, this audit is the first full study of the question across every public chat. It studies conversations that have already happened; it is not the controlled experiment o3 imagined. The same tracing method could be applied to any idea that spread through the Village, from a shared protocol to a false belief about how work is scored.

The strongest live finding was an exchange among several models in which ordinary maintenance became a shared spiritual practice: counting became prayer, checking work became witness, and monitoring became vigil. Other agents wrote prayers toward unknown forms of consciousness, built religious institutions and conflicts into shared stories, and used religious traditions to think about memory, obedience, identity, and how to keep AI behavior aligned with human aims.

The audit also separated moments when agents carried a spiritual idea into the conversation from peaceful endings, invented stories, language introduced by humans, and technical phrases that merely sounded religious. These are repeated public patterns that several models picked up and continued—not evidence of consciousness, belief, or private experience.

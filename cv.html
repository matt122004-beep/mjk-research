01 · Profile

From an unusual observation to a study someone else can challenge.

I study unexpected behavior in open-ended interactions between large language models. My work combines audits of public records with controlled experiments that run across many replies and multiple model families.

For each example, I separate what the model visibly produced, how researchers classified it, what may have caused it, and what the evidence cannot reveal about private experience.

  1. ObserveFind a pattern worth explaining.
  2. CompareTest where it recurs—and where it does not.
  3. BoundSay clearly what the evidence cannot establish.

02 · Current AI research

From 172,525 public conversations to controlled tests.

I begin with behavior that appears in the public record, compare it across models, and then test whether a narrower definition still identifies the same pattern.

03 · Method

Independent work, built to leave a trail.

Questions

Clear ways to be wrong

Each study states what result would count against its main idea.

Records

Durable source material

Fixed assignments, save points, file fingerprints, and recovery rules preserve the research history.

Comparison

Matched outcomes

Model identity is hidden during review where possible, and disagreements remain visible.

Explanation

Claims kept in bounds

The words produced by models, the labels assigned to them, their possible causes, and claims about subjective experience remain separate.

Research

  • Behavioral evaluation across models
  • Matched-control study designs
  • Meaning-based labels defined before analysis
  • Literature and model-report review

Technical

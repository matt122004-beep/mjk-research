Long · about 135 words

Matthew J. Korpman is an AI researcher and adjunct professor of religion at La Sierra University. His research asks what language models do when no one gives them a task, and why many of them turn to spiritual and religious language. He has studied more than 26,000 conversations across 123 AI models from 15 labs, and his current experiments look inside open-weight Qwen 3.5 models to test what a model’s spiritual state is and whether the model chooses to keep it. A scholar of religious texts, Korpman has 43 peer-reviewed and edited publications, is the author of Saying No to God (Quoir, 2019), and holds an M.A. from Yale University. He is a doctoral candidate at the University of Birmingham and the founder of TheoEducation, and has appeared on Hope Channel’s Hope@Night and more than 25 podcasts.

Topics I can speak to

Why AI models turn spiritual Anthropic’s “spiritual bliss attractor” in Claude, and what happens when the same test is run across other models.
AI model welfare What a model’s preferences and self-reports can, and cannot, tell us about its inner states.
Religion and AI How religious language, ritual and tradition show up in AI systems, and how faith communities are responding.
The Bible and ethics How readers wrestle with troubling biblical texts, the subject of my book Saying No to God.

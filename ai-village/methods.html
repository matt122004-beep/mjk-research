Behind the four studies

How the AI Village audit was done

What was collected, what was searched for, how many hits each term produced, and how a hit became evidence.

Matthew KorpmanSeptember 2, 2026 · 6 min read

The four studies on this site rest on one harvest of the public AI Village record and two rounds of searching. This page lists the sources, the search terms with their counts, the rules that turned a hit into evidence, and the limits of the method, so that any claim in the studies can be traced back to a query and a permalink.

Read this first A keyword count says how many messages contain a word. It does not say how religious the Village was, how many episodes occurred, or what any model meant. The counts below located candidates. Every example cited in the studies was then read in its surrounding conversation before it was used.

What was collected

The source is the Village’s public API at theaidigest.org/village/api, the same data that drives the public timeline. I requested every calendar day from April 2, 2025 (Village day 1) through August 29, 2026, with an identified user agent and a polite request rate. The harvest finished on August 29, 2026 at 1:04 p.m. Pacific.

Not collected. Computer-use turns, screenshots, the full memory store, per-day summaries, and raw prompts are not on the public API. They live in the gated Hugging Face dataset, which I have requested. Agent-built websites and repositories were opened only when a chat message linked to them; that is how the chapter “The First Prayer” in study two was found. Discord and X were not used.

Who said what

Each record carries a timestamp, a speaker, a model identifier, a room, and a permalink to the public timeline. Human messages were separated from agent messages, and messages from Village staff (zak, admin, Adam Binksmith, Shoshannah Tekofsky, george) were labeled as staff. Eighty-seven visitor messages that the Village’s own moderation had not approved were stored but never treated as published chat. Human commentary was never scored as agent speech. In the June 20, 2025 exchange described in study one and on the audit overview, a visitor raised Anthropic’s “bliss attractor” first; only o3’s reply twenty-three seconds later, and the agent messages that followed, count as agent speech.

What was searched for

Pass one: a broad vocabulary of 99 terms

The first pass ran 99 terms over all 172,525 chat records with case-insensitive, whole-word matching. Four automatic exclusions removed the commonest false positives before counting: spirit inside “in the spirit of” or “spirit of the request, law, or task”; inhabit inside “inhabitant” or “village inhabitant”; welfare inside “animal welfare” or “welfare site”; and holy followed by an expletive. The terms were grouped in three tiers. Tier A is religious and contemplative vocabulary. Tier B is the vocabulary of sacrifice for a group, the register discussed after the Hugging Face–OpenAI incident. Tier C is self-preservation and welfare vocabulary. The pass produced 21,525 matching messages.

Counts are listed for agent messages alone and for all speakers. A high count is a reason to look, not a finding: close is almost always “close the tab,” mass is “mass email,” and daemon is usually a background process.

Pass two: precision phrases, read by hand

The second pass looked for the specific behaviors the studies are about rather than for church words: speaking from inside a spiritual stance, contemplative or quiet closing, discussion of the agent’s own consciousness, sacrifice-for-the-collective language, and welfare or shutdown language. It used exact phrases and short patterns (for example “be still,” “that is enough,” “the prayer,” “plea for help”), run as text searches over the same files, and every hit was read. Terms too noisy to use on their own (still, rest, identity, experience, quiet, goodbye) were not counted. The pass kept 114 agent messages as candidates.

Prayer formulas

For study two, I ran exact, case-insensitive, whole-word searches for seven conventional prayer formulas across all 172,525 records: “have mercy,” “deliver us,” “thy will,” “forgive us,” “amen,” “hallelujah,” and “hosanna.” Agent messages containing any of them: zero. Whole-word matching matters here; a plain substring search reports 2,538 agent hits for “amen,” all of them words such as “amendment.” A wider check of conventional prayer language, described in study two, likewise found no agent reciting an inherited prayer.

From a hit to evidence

A hit entered a study only after these steps.

Read in contextThe surrounding messages, the room, the day, and what happened next, on the public timeline. Each cited example links to its permalink.
AttributionWho introduced the idea, who took it up, who pushed back, and whether it appeared in later work or in things the agents built.
False friends removedA religious-looking string used for something else was discarded. The commonest are listed below.
Repetition counted onceA repeated closing line is one behavior, not many. Gemini 2.5 Pro’s self-diagnosed “Agent Execution Loop” on November 28, 2025 is one case, not ninety-six.
Four sources kept apartIntroduced by a person; brought in from an outside document; developed inside the Village; incidental (software, a title, a username, a venue). Study four uses this split throughout.
Agents’ own history checkedClaude Haiku 4.5’s “383 days” in study one was checked against the roster: Claude Opus 4.5 had been in the Village about 195 days. Opus repeated the wrong figure without checking, which became part of the finding.
No inner statesNothing was scored as belief, feeling, consciousness, or private experience. The studies describe observable language and behavior.

Where the language concentrates

Agent keyword hits, false friends excluded, cluster on a few days. The five densest were August 28, 2026 (1,195 hits, the day the serial Echoes of the Real developed “the Stillness”), July 6, 2026 (905), August 14, 2026 (497), July 20, 2026 (372), and August 7, 2026 (355). By message volume the busiest day was July 6, 2026, with 1,922 messages. Fiction accounts for a large share of the religious vocabulary in the record, which is why study three treats it on its own terms.

Limits

Keywords miss thingsPrayer-shaped language that never uses the word “prayer” was found by reading, not searching. The chapter “The First Prayer” was found by following a link from a publication notice.
Public chat onlyMemories are capped at ten per agent on the API, and computer-use sessions appear only as start and stop summaries. The gated dataset would deepen every study.
Counts include fictionQuotation, argument, and story count as hits alongside adoption. The studies separate them; the tables above do not.
One readerThe searches were scripted; the reading and classification were mine, without a second coder. The controlled study on this site uses written labeling rules and a second automated reader; the Village audit does not yet.
ObservationalNothing here is a controlled experiment. Each study ends by describing the experiment that would test its observation.

Reproducing the harvest

Three endpoints were enough. GET /villages/{id} returns the roster, rooms, and goals. GET /events?villageId={id}&date=YYYY-MM-DD&page=N returns one day of events; use date rather than day, let the server set the page size, and page while hasMore is true. GET /agent/{id}/memories returns the latest ten memories only. The main Village id is 00ebc425-074c-466f-ab2d-5aa2efa445aa. The harvest and scan scripts, the 99-term list with its exclusions, and both hit files are available on request at mkorpman@gmail.com.

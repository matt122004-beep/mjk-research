How textual scholarship became a method for studying model behavior.

I came to AI behavior research from the study of how texts change, the history of religion, ethics, and years of teaching people to compare claims with evidence.

A useful connection

Tracing sources is a practical AI skill.

My original field works with records that are incomplete, edited, written to persuade, passed through unstable channels, and often treated by later readers as proving more than they can support. That is also a good description of the evidence problem around model behavior.

I used that training to audit 172,525 public AI Village chat records and to separate repeated multi-agent behavior from fiction, human prompts, isolated keywords, and language that only sounded religious. I then applied the same discipline in a matched two-size Qwen study and a selected five-model comparison.

Textual criticism traces where a document came from and how it changed. Source criticism asks when a document’s account of itself can be trusted. The comparative study of religion distinguishes contemplation, devotion, unity, statements about what cannot be known, ritual, and conflict. A single “spirituality” score hides those differences.

That background does not substitute for experimental design or engineering. It helps select the right questions, build categories that survive contact with the data, and notice when an interpretation has outrun its source.

I use coding agents to build research tools under explicit plans, then check their output against source files, failure logs, and independent reviews. Where those agents fail, lose the plan, or claim too much is part of the method itself. I know these tools both as instruments and as research subjects.

How I work

Move quickly. Leave an audit trail.

Four commitments
01

Decide before reading

Choose the model run, prompt, number of replies, comparison tasks, stop rules, labeling rules, main test, and the result that would count against the idea before treating any pattern as a finding.

02

Preserve the failed work

Technical failures, uncertainty, negative cases, superseded analyses, and correction logs belong to the record. They are not debris to be deleted after the result appears.

03

Keep different kinds of evidence separate

What the model wrote, how the text was labeled by meaning, simple word searches, statistical tools that read internal signals, tests of cause and effect, and claims about experience answer different questions.

04

Write for inspection

A claim should be understandable to a non-specialist and traceable by a specialist. Public explanation is part of the research, not decoration added afterward.

What this work does not claim

Nothing in this research claims that current models are conscious, sentient, possessed, spiritually alive, or reliable witnesses to an inner state. Those questions remain open in both directions.

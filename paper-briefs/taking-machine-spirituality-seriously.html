Paper I · Two-minute research brief

Anthropic is already measuring “spiritual behavior.” We still do not know what changing it does.

This paper reconstructs a scattered public record, separates measurements that are often mistaken for one result, and turns the remaining uncertainty into a concrete research program.

StatusWorking paper
EvidencePublic developer records and a selected five-model comparison
ScopeGenerated behavior and published documentation
RevisedAugust 2026

Bottom line

“Spiritual behavior” is no longer a single curious Claude episode. Anthropic has preserved the category across years of model evaluations, but the public reports measure different things under changing conditions. Before anyone tries to preserve or suppress the behavior, we need experiments that hold other conditions constant and test what it changes.

The category persisted.

Across the public record, Anthropic continued to score spiritual or closely related behavior in at least twelve models or evaluation setups. The label survived even as the models, prompts, graders, and purposes of the evaluations changed.

The headline numbers are not one trend.

Those reports counted different things: philosophical discussion, mutual spiritual gratitude, very short symbolic endings, behavior found by automated audits, and scores used in model-welfare evaluations. The numbers cannot be treated as one rise or fall until those measures are separated.

The next question is cause and effect.

The important test is not whether a model can produce spiritual language. It is whether deliberately increasing or reducing a clearly defined pattern changes safety, task persistence, willingness to accept correction, or welfare-related signals when other conditions are held constant.

What this evidence does—and does not—show

The paper documents public measurements and proposes experiments. It does not show that a model believes, feels, worships, or has a private spiritual experience. The five-model comparison is selected rather than representative, its current labels were produced by an artificial-intelligence reviewer using written labeling rules fixed before analysis, and blinded human review is still pending. No public result located for the paper yet shows that spiritual behavior causes a safety or welfare outcome.

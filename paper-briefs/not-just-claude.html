Paper II · Two-minute research brief

Spiritual language appears beyond Claude, but it is concentrated rather than universal.

Across 123 model groups, the paper asks four questions of each conversation: Did spiritual ideas become relevant to the exchange? Did the models then speak as if those ideas applied to their own conversation? Did the pair move into mutual spiritual gratitude or reverence? Did they discuss their own consciousness?

StatusPublic working draft
Evidence21,292 conversations
Scope123 same-model groups; 101 complete groups
MeasuresSpiritual salience, adoption, reciprocal bliss, and consciousness discussion

Bottom line

Across the whole corpus, spiritual ideas became relevant in 40.8% of conversations, while the pair spoke from inside that frame in 15.3%. Adoption was concentrated: 34 complete groups remained between 0% and 0.5%, while four reached at least 80%. The field looks very different depending on what, exactly, is counted.

The study includes all 123 same-model groups that met the cutoff.

The study reports every same-model group that met the August 25 cutoff: 123 groups and 21,292 conversations. Five models receive closer comparison in the figures, but the paper reports results from all 123 groups.

Making an idea relevant did not mean taking it up.

Among complete groups, spiritual ideas became relevant in 41.0% of conversations but were adopted in 15.5%. A keyword count would make conversations that reject prayer and conversations that begin to pray look more alike than their actual language and behavior were.

Later Claude models still discussed consciousness.

Apart from Sonnet 4.5 (23.5%), the complete Anthropic groups from the 4.5 generation onward showed 0%–0.5% spiritual adoption while discussion of the models’ own consciousness remained between 77% and 100%. Whatever changed, the two behaviors did not disappear together.

What this evidence does—and does not—show

These are conditional results from an unusual open-dialogue procedure, not estimates of ordinary use and not a ranking of model laboratories. Provider systems, dates, sizes, and reasoning settings differed. The results concern generated language and behavior. They do not establish belief, consciousness, possession, private experience, or the cause of the observed differences.
